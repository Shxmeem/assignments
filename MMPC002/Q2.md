# Fixing Talent Shortages and Turnover in an Expanding Multinational

## Introduction

When a company expands quickly, hiring becomes a race to fill empty seats. Job descriptions are copied from old ones, interviews are rushed, and new employees are left to figure things out alone. The result is too few suitable people, people who do not fit the work, and people who leave within a year or two.

This assignment treats these as symptoms of one weakness: hiring and planning are not connected. It proposes a model that links planning, job analysis, recruitment, selection and socialisation into one process, and shows how it supports stability and adaptability.

## Diagnosing the Problem

**Talent shortage.** Shortages often come from poor planning, not from a lack of people in the market. If the company does not know what skills it needs next year, it searches only when the need is urgent, which is slow and costly. It also looks only for ready-made experts and ignores people who could be trained.

**Skill mismatch.** This usually starts with an inaccurate job description. If nobody has checked what the job involves, the company advertises for the wrong skills and hires people who struggle. Across countries it gets worse, as the same job title can mean different work in different places.

**High turnover.** Many exits trace back to hiring. Some people were promised a job that turned out different, some were hired for skills they rarely use, and some were ignored in their first months. These are process failures, though they are often blamed on "unreliable employees".

The three problems feed each other: mismatch causes early exits, exits create shortages, and shortages lead to rushed hiring and more mismatches. The model is built to break this loop.

## The Proposed Model

The model has four connected stages. The output of each is the input of the next, so they run as one system.

### Stage 1: Plan (human resource planning)

- *Look ahead.* Each year HR and business leaders list where the company is expanding and what skills it will need over the next two to three years.
- *Count what exists.* The company records employees' skills, including those not used in their current jobs.
- *Close the gap.* For each gap it chooses between training, moving people, hiring or contractors. Hiring is not the default.
- *Watch the leaving rate.* HR tracks where and why people leave, by department, country and length of service.

### Stage 2: Define (job analysis)

- *Study the real work.* For important roles, HR talks to people doing the job and their managers to learn the tasks, essential skills and conditions.
- *Separate must-have from nice-to-have.* Skills that can be learned on the job are listed apart, so capable people are not rejected for minor gaps.
- *Use common descriptions with local changes.* One core description per role, adjusted locally, allows comparison and moves between countries.
- *Keep them current.* Descriptions are reviewed when work changes.

### Stage 3: Attract and Choose (recruitment and selection)

- *Look in more places.* Use colleges, internships, employee referrals, returning former employees and people from other industries with transferable skills.
- *Be honest.* Advertisements and interviews describe the work, pay, hours and difficulties as they are. This may reduce applicants but increases the number who stay.
- *Test what the job needs.* Use practical tasks and structured interviews with the same questions for every candidate. Trained interviewers score candidates before discussing them together, which reduces bias.
- *Judge fit and learning ability.* A fast learner is often a better long-term choice than someone with exactly the right experience today.
- *Hire locally, to shared standards.* Country teams run their own hiring, so they know the local market and keep the same quality of selection.
- *Check the process.* HR reviews whether selection predicted good performance and whether any group is rejected without a work-related reason.

### Stage 4: Settle (socialisation)

- *Prepare before day one.* New employees get clear information about their role, team and first tasks.
- *Structure the first months.* For three to six months each person has a plan of who to meet, what to learn and what is expected, with regular manager conversations.
- *Assign a buddy.* An experienced colleague, not the manager, helps with daily questions and, for international moves, practical and cultural matters.
- *Check early.* HR contacts new employees at about one, three and six months, so warning signs are fixed before the person resigns.
- *Show a path.* Employees see how they can grow and move between roles or countries.

**How the stages connect.** Planning tells the company what to look for, job analysis turns that into accurate roles, selection finds matching people, and socialisation helps them settle. Exit data and early feedback then flow back to show where planning, roles or selection went wrong. This makes the model a loop that improves, not a one-time project.

## Justification: Sustainability and Adaptability

**Long-term sustainability.** The model reduces dependence on emergency hiring. Planning builds skills before they are urgently needed. Honest recruitment and careful selection mean fewer first-year exits, which saves repeated hiring and training costs and keeps knowledge in the company.

**Workforce adaptability.** The model records skills, not only job titles, so the company can see who can move into new work. It selects for learning ability, so new hires can adjust when tasks change. It also treats training and internal moves as the first option when a gap appears.

**Fairness and trust.** Structured selection and regular checks for unfair patterns help the company treat people consistently across countries.

## Limitations

The model costs time and money, and structured hiring is slower than quick hiring. It can start with the hardest-to-fill roles and extend later. It also depends on managers in every country following the process and on accurate data. Some turnover comes from pay, poor managers or better offers elsewhere, which this model will not solve. Forecasts of future skills will sometimes be wrong, so the plan needs regular review.

## Conclusion

Talent shortages, skill mismatches and high turnover are not separate problems. They come from planning that does not look ahead, poorly defined roles, rushed hiring and new employees left without support. Treating them one at a time only moves the problem around.

The proposed model connects planning, job analysis, recruitment, selection and socialisation, and uses information from each stage to improve the others. It helps the company keep people longer, fill gaps from within, and respond when needs change. It takes patience and honest data, but a company that builds its workforce deliberately is stronger than one always hiring in a hurry.

# Digital Technology and Business Communication

## Introduction

Not long ago, a query to a supplier meant a phone call or a visit. Now it may go out as an email, a WhatsApp message, or a video call booked an hour earlier. Digital tools have made communication quicker and cheaper, but they have also created new ways to be misunderstood. This assignment looks at how digital technology has changed business communication and weighs the advantages and disadvantages of email, social networks and online meetings.

## 1. How Digital Technology Has Changed Business Communication

The biggest change is speed. A message that once took days by post now arrives in seconds, and a firm in Kochi can negotiate with a buyer in Dubai without anyone travelling. Digital messages also leave a record, so it is easier to check who agreed to what.

The same tools bring problems. Staff are interrupted all day, tone is often lost in text, and company information is exposed to theft and fraud. Digital tools help most when people choose the right one for each task.

## 2. Email

**Advantages**
- It creates a written record. A purchase manager who asks three suppliers for quotations by email can later prove which price each one gave.
- Documents, invoices and photographs can be attached.
- The cost is almost nothing, even when writing to hundreds of people.
- The reader can answer when convenient, which suits people in different time zones.

**Disadvantages**
- Inboxes overflow, and important messages get buried.
- Tone is easily misread. A short reply meant to be efficient can sound rude.
- Careless use causes trouble, such as a reply-all that sends a private remark to the whole company.
- Fraud is a real risk. In 2015, Ubiquiti Networks disclosed that it had lost about $46.7 million after employees were tricked by fake emails posing as executives and vendors.

## 3. Social Networks

**Advantages**
- A business can speak to customers directly, without waiting for newspapers or advertisements.
- Feedback comes quickly. A bad review or a happy comment shows up within hours.
- Costs are low compared with print or television.
- LinkedIn and similar sites help firms find staff and help candidates find firms.

Amul is a well-known example. Its quick, witty posts on current events keep a decades-old dairy brand in everyday conversation. Zomato also replies to customers in public in a casual voice, which has made its page worth following.

**Disadvantages**
- Complaints become public. In 2009, musician Dave Carroll posted a song video after United Airlines damaged his guitar and refused to compensate him. It was watched millions of times and embarrassed the airline.
- A careless post is hard to take back, since screenshots last long after deletion.
- Staff may mix personal opinions with the company's voice.
- Serious matters, such as a contract dispute, do not belong in a comment thread.

## 4. Online Meetings

**Advantages**
- Travel time and cost disappear. A manager can meet a client and a vendor in the same afternoon from one desk.
- People from several cities can join at once.
- Screen sharing lets everyone look at the same sheet or design, and a recording helps those who could not attend.
- Some firms rely on it fully. GitLab, a software company, has no offices and runs on online meetings and written documents.

**Disadvantages**
- Poor internet breaks the flow, and a frozen screen at the wrong moment wastes everyone's time.
- Long sessions tire people. A 2021 Stanford paper on "Zoom fatigue" pointed to constant eye contact on screen and the strain of watching one's own face.
- Body language is harder to read, so quieter members may say nothing, and the manager never learns they disagreed.
- Meetings multiply because they are so easy to schedule.
- Different time zones mean someone always joins early or late.

## 5. Choosing the Right Tool

A good rule is to match the tool to the task. Email suits quotations, confirmations and anything that needs a record. Social networks suit public announcements and customer conversations. Online meetings suit discussions, disagreements and sensitive news, where tone matters. Bad news, such as a delayed order, should not be sent as a comment or a one-line email.

## Conclusion

Digital technology has made business communication faster, wider in reach and cheaper, and each tool discussed here has a clear use. Each also has a cost: email can overload and mislead, social networks can expose a company's mistakes, and online meetings can tire people and hide disagreement. Organisations get the best results when they decide in advance which tool fits which message, and when staff remember that a faster message is not always a clearer one.

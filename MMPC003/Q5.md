# Question

What is Goods and Service Tax (GST)? Explain the salient features and advantages of GST.

# Introduction

Before 2017, a product sold in India could face many different taxes, collected by both the central and state governments. A business had to deal with several tax laws, forms and offices, and the same product was often taxed again and again as it moved from one stage to the next. To end this confusion, India brought in the Goods and Services Tax. This assignment explains what GST is, its main features and its advantages.

# Meaning of GST

Goods and Services Tax is an **indirect tax** charged on the supply of goods and services. It replaced many earlier taxes with one tax across the country. It came into force on **1 July 2017** after the Constitution was amended for this purpose. GST is collected at every stage of the supply chain, from the maker to the final buyer, but at each stage the seller pays tax only on the value added at that stage. In the end, the burden falls on the final consumer.

The taxes that it took in include, at the central level, central excise duty, service tax and additional customs duty, and at the state level, value added tax, entry tax, luxury tax, entertainment tax and octroi.

# Salient Features of GST

1. **One tax on goods and services.** Earlier, goods and services were taxed under different laws. Now a single tax covers the supply of both.

2. **Dual structure.** India is a federal country, so both the Centre and the states can tax. GST is therefore collected in two parts. When goods or services are sold within one state, the buyer pays **Central GST (CGST)** to the Centre and **State GST (SGST)** to the state. For union territories without a legislature, **Union Territory GST** applies. When they are sold from one state to another, or imported, **Integrated GST (IGST)** is charged, and it is collected by the Centre and shared with the state where the goods are consumed.

3. **Destination based.** The tax goes to the state where the goods or services are used, and not to the state where they are made. This is different from the old system, in which the producing state often gained.

4. **Input tax credit.** A business that pays GST on its purchases can set that amount off against the GST it has to pay on its sales. For example, if a shop pays 100 as tax on goods it buys and collects 130 as tax on its sales, it pays only 30 to the government. This ensures that tax is paid only on the value added and not on the full price at each stage.

5. **The GST Council.** This is a body headed by the Union Finance Minister, with the finance ministers of all the states as members. It decides on rates, exemptions, rules and other matters. Decisions need agreement of a large majority, with the Centre and states each holding votes. This keeps the Centre and the states working together.

6. **Several tax rates.** GST is charged at different rates depending on the type of goods and services, with lower rates for items of daily need and higher rates for luxury and harmful goods. Some essential items such as fresh vegetables and milk are exempt. The rates are reviewed and changed from time to time by the Council.

7. **Common online system.** Registration, filing of returns and payment of tax are done through a common online portal. This makes the process the same in every state.

8. **Limit for small businesses.** Small businesses with turnover below a set limit do not have to register. Those slightly above it can choose a composition scheme, in which they pay a small fixed percentage of turnover and have simpler filing.

9. **Some items are outside GST.** Petroleum products such as petrol and diesel, natural gas, crude oil, alcohol for drinking and electricity are not yet under it, and are taxed by the states and the Centre in the old way.

10. **Zero rating of exports.** Exports are not taxed under GST, and exporters can claim a refund of the tax paid on their inputs, which keeps Indian goods competitive abroad.

# Advantages of GST

1. **Ends the cascading effect.** Under the old system, tax was paid on tax. With input credit, tax is charged only on value added, which lowers the final cost of goods.

2. **One national market.** Earlier, each state had its own tax rates and rules, and goods moving between states faced extra taxes. Now the country is one market, and a business can sell in any state under the same system.

3. **Simpler tax system.** Many taxes, forms and offices were replaced with one law and one online portal, which saves the time and effort of businesses.

4. **Lower cost of moving goods.** Check posts at state borders, where trucks waited for hours, were removed. Goods now move faster, and transport and storage costs fall. Firms can also plan warehouses based on business needs, not on tax differences between states.

5. **Greater honesty and transparency.** Because credit is claimed only when the supplier has paid the tax and filed returns, businesses have a reason to buy from honest suppliers. Online records make it harder to hide sales, so tax evasion falls.

6. **Higher revenue for the government.** As more businesses come into the tax net and evasion falls, the government collects more, which can be spent on public services.

7. **Benefit to consumers.** Lower costs and lower tax on many items can reduce prices. A single tax also makes it easier to see how much tax one is paying.

8. **Help for small businesses and exporters.** The composition scheme and the exemption limit lighten the load on small firms, and the refund on exports helps exporters compete.

9. **Growth of the economy.** A simple, uniform tax, lower costs and a larger market encourage investment, production and job creation.

# Conclusion

GST is a single tax on goods and services, collected in the same way across India through a dual structure under the guidance of the GST Council. Its input tax credit, common market and online system have made taxation simpler and more transparent, and it removes the tax on tax that raised prices earlier.

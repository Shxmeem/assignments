# Digital and Social Media Marketing: Building Engagement and Brand Awareness

Not long ago, a company reached its customers through newspapers, hoardings and television, and it learned very little about how they responded. Digital marketing changed this. It means promoting products through the internet and connected devices, using channels such as search engines, websites, email, messaging apps and social media. Social media marketing is one part of it, focused on platforms like Instagram, YouTube, Facebook and X. This assignment explains how both help a business become known and keep its customers involved, and then discusses two tools used by Indian organisations.

## Role in Building Brand Awareness

Awareness is the first step in selling anything, because nobody buys a brand they have never heard of. Digital channels make this step cheaper and easier to measure. A small business can place an advertisement before people of a chosen age and city for a modest daily amount, and it can see within hours how many people viewed it. In traditional media the same company would have paid for a broad audience, most of whom were never going to buy.

Search visibility matters as well. When someone types a query into a search engine, the brands that appear on the first page get noticed and are often trusted more. A firm with a well-kept website and useful content gets found by people who are already looking for what it sells.

Social media adds reach through sharing. A post that people find funny or useful travels beyond the company's own followers, and this free circulation costs nothing. Video has become especially strong here, since a short clip can show a product in use more clearly than a paragraph of text.

## Role in Customer Engagement

Engagement means that customers do more than buy once. They respond, comment, ask questions, recommend the brand and return. Digital marketing makes this a two-way conversation. A customer who has a complaint can post it publicly, and a company that replies quickly and politely often turns an angry buyer into a loyal one. Replies are visible to everyone, so other customers see how the firm behaves.

Companies also learn from this conversation. Comments, reviews and poll results show what people like and what annoys them, and this feedback can be used to improve products and service. Email and messaging allow personal follow-up, such as order updates or a reminder about a product left in the cart. Offers can be sent only to those who are likely to want them, which keeps customers from feeling flooded with irrelevant messages.

There are risks. A careless post can spread quickly and damage a reputation, and customers soon notice when a brand only talks about itself. Engagement needs steady attention and an honest tone.

## Tool One: Social Media Platforms

The first tool is the regular use of platforms such as Instagram and X to publish content and speak with customers. Amul is a well-known case. Its advertisements, featuring the Amul girl, have commented on current events for decades, and the brand continues the habit online by posting topical illustrations soon after news breaks. People share these because they enjoy them, so the brand stays in view with a small budget. The tone is light, the message is consistent, and nothing in it feels forced.

Zomato offers another example. Its posts and notifications use humour and everyday language, and its social media team often replies to users in the same style. This has given the company a recognisable personality in a market where food delivery apps otherwise look alike. The lesson from both firms is that a distinct voice, kept up over time, earns attention that paid advertising alone cannot.

## Tool Two: WhatsApp Business

The second tool is WhatsApp Business, which suits India because the app is already used by a very large number of people for daily conversation. It lets a firm create a business profile, display a product catalogue, send order confirmations and answer queries in a familiar chat window. Customers do not have to download a new app or learn a new process.

JioMart used this in 2020 when it began taking grocery orders through WhatsApp, which allowed customers to place orders from a chat rather than a separate shopping app. Many small shopkeepers, tailors and home bakers in towns across the country also use catalogues and broadcast lists to share new stock and take orders from regular customers. For them it costs very little and builds a direct link with buyers. The main caution is that messages should be sent only to people who agreed to receive them, since unwanted messages quickly lead to blocked numbers.

## Conclusion

Digital and social media marketing help a business in two connected ways. They make a brand visible to the right people at a manageable cost, and they keep a conversation going after the sale. Amul and Zomato show how a steady voice on social platforms builds recognition, while JioMart and small retailers show how a simple messaging tool can bring customers closer. In each case the results come from consistency and honest communication, not from spending the most money.

# Confidence Interval for the Difference in Mean Years of Experience

## Introduction

An industry-wide survey recorded how many years of sales-related experience male and female sales managers have. We want a 95% confidence interval for µ₁ − µ₂, where µ₁ is the true mean experience of males and µ₂ is the true mean experience of females. The interval gives a range of plausible values for how much the two groups differ on average, which is more informative than the sample difference alone.

## Data

| | Males (group 1) | Females (group 2) |
|---|---|---|
| Sample size | n₁ = 80 | n₂ = 70 |
| Sample mean | x̄₁ = 21.7 | x̄₂ = 18.5 |
| Standard deviation | s₁ = 9.3 | s₂ = 4.8 |

## Step 1: Choosing the method

The two samples are independent, since they come from different people. Both are large (80 and 70, well above 30), so the Central Limit Theorem lets us treat the sample means as approximately normal. The sample standard deviations are quite different (9.3 versus 4.8), so we should not assume the population variances are equal. We therefore use the unpooled standard error and, because the samples are large, the z critical value for 95% confidence, 1.96.

(A t-based interval with Welch's degrees of freedom would give almost the same answer here. Using z is standard for large samples.)

## Step 2: Point estimate

x̄₁ − x̄₂ = 21.7 − 18.5 = 3.2 years

On average, the males in the sample have 3.2 more years of experience.

## Step 3: Standard error

SE = √(s₁²/n₁ + s₂²/n₂)

- s₁²/n₁ = 9.3² / 80 = 86.49 / 80 = 1.0811
- s₂²/n₂ = 4.8² / 70 = 23.04 / 70 = 0.3291

SE = √(1.0811 + 0.3291) = √1.4102 ≈ 1.1875

## Step 4: Margin of error

Margin of error = z × SE = 1.96 × 1.1875 ≈ 2.33

## Step 5: The interval

3.2 ± 2.33

- Lower limit: 3.2 − 2.33 = 0.87
- Upper limit: 3.2 + 2.33 = 5.53

**95% confidence interval for µ₁ − µ₂: (0.87, 5.53) years**

## Interpretation

We are 95% confident that the true difference in mean experience between male and female sales managers lies between about 0.87 and 5.53 years, with males having more. This means that if we repeated the survey many times and built an interval each time, about 95% of those intervals would contain the true difference.

Because the whole interval is above zero, the data suggest the male mean really is higher than the female mean, and the gap is not just sampling noise. The interval is fairly wide, though, so the size of the gap is not pinned down precisely: it could be less than a year or more than five.

## Conclusion

The sample difference is 3.2 years, with a standard error of about 1.19 and a margin of error of about 2.33. The 95% confidence interval for µ₁ − µ₂ is (0.87, 5.53) years. Since zero is not in the interval, there is evidence that male sales managers in this industry have more years of sales-related experience on average than female sales managers. This only describes the difference in experience; the survey data alone cannot explain why it exists.
